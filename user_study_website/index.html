Personalized Robot Feeding User Study

Thank you for participating in our study! Robot-assisted feeding systems can improve the quality of life for care recipients with mobility limitations and reduce caregiver workload. Your participation can help us design more comfortable and intuitive feeding behaviors.

The assistive feeding robot system you'll be evaluating in this study.

People eat in a variety of contexts—personal settings, while watching TV, or at social gatherings—and may have different expectations for how the robot should behave depending upon the context. In this study, we evaluate the robot's ability to predict your preferences in new meal contexts based on what it has learned from earlier interactions. We focus on:

  • Food-Dip Combinations: Which dips you prefer for specific food items. Example: Dip apple slices in caramel, but feed watermelon plain.
  • Transfer Cue: The signal the robot waits for after picking up a bite:
    • Open Mouth: Wait until you open your mouth.
    • Button: Wait until you press a button.
    • Auto-Continue: Transfer automatically without waiting.
  • Verbal Prompts: Whether the robot should verbally announce its actions, such as saying "Please open your mouth when ready" or "Please press the button when ready" after picking up a bite.
  • Visual Occlusion: Which side(s) of you should remain unobstructed and whether the robot is blocking those sides. Example: In a social setting with a friend on your left, minimize occlusion on that side by repositioning the plate to the right.

You will see five mealtime contexts. For each one:

  1. Compare two sets of robot-predicted preferences—one that incorporates your past preferences and context, and one that does not.
  2. Indicate your true preferences for food-dip combinations, transfer cue, verbal prompts, and visual occlusion.
